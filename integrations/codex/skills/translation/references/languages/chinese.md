# Chinese notes

Into Chinese (Simplified unless the brief says otherwise) and out of it. The typographic conventions come from subtitling style guides and a client's guide overrides them; the rest are points about the English-Chinese pair from translation textbooks and studies of machine output. This is not a complete style guide for Chinese.

## Punctuation and typography

- **Wrap work titles in 《》.** Albums, films, songs, TV programmes, newspapers, magazines,
  books and works of art take the title marks.
  Example: 你看过《复仇者联盟》这部电影吗？

## Numbers, dates and units

- **In subtitles, use Arabic numerals for factual numbers.** Addresses, phone numbers, dates,
  times, measurements, chapter and page numbers and units take them; spell small counts in
  characters where space allows (五千二百, 四亿三千万); never mix the two systems in one
  series; do not abbreviate years. Example: 她1992年来过.

## Grammar carried over from the source

- **Do not mirror an English passive in Chinese.** Work out what it does (suppress the agent,
  distance the writer, mark adversity) and use the Chinese device for that function: an active
  clause with a generic subject, an impersonal or reflexive form. A formal passive can import a
  meaning the source never had.
- **Leave out categories Chinese does not mark.** Do not encode number, tense or gender unless
  the passage depends on it (a title, a count, a contrast); repeated optional marking reads as
  unnatural. Example: 'China's Panda Reserves' becomes 中国的熊猫保护区, with the number open.
- **Keep the adverse form where the source enacts evasion.** Where the source conveys
  cowardice, evasion or denial of responsibility, the Chinese distancing passive may be right.
  Example: Le Carré's 'Note was taken' passage, where the passives enact evasion.
- **Carry definiteness by position, not by a determiner.** A nominal before the main verb
  tends to be definite, one after it may be new; adjust word order rather than adding an
  article Chinese lacks.

## Words and phrases

- **Build Chinese slogans to the Chinese pattern.** A literal rendering fails even when the
  words are right; the pattern is rhyming pairs of four-character phrases. 'finger-lickin'
  good' as 好食到吃手指 fails; 吮指回味，自在滋味 works.

## Sentences and text organisation

- **Reorder enumerations to the Chinese sequence.** Size-based lists run large to small and
  addresses country-first, the reverse of English; keep events chronological unless you add
  explicit signals. Example: 'GPs, a pharmacist, a local dentist, and ... hospital clinics at
  Exmouth and the Royal Devon and Exeter Hospital' is reordered to name the hospitals first.

## Names, terms and abbreviations

- **Separate transliterated name parts with the full-width interpunct.** 威廉·莎士比亚;
  omit it for Korean and Japanese names, and drop it when the source gives only initials
  ('T.K.' becomes TK).
- **Keep nicknames readable as nicknames.** Use one consistent name, or turn a short form into
  a natural Chinese nickname (丽贝卡/小丽), never a phonetic transliteration of the English
  clipping (丽贝卡/贝克斯).

## Legal and official texts

- **Choose the Chinese term for 'law' by sense.** English 'law' covers natural order and
  enacted law, which Chinese separates (li for the order of nature, li for rites, fa for
  human-made law, zhi for control); a default rendering makes a conceptual claim the source
  did not.

## Subtitles and dubbing

- **Strip prose punctuation from Chinese subtitles.** Replace commas and full stops with a
  single space; keep the enumeration comma only inside lists; type ellipses as … (U+2026, not
  U+22EF); keep question marks. Example: clauses split by a space, lists written A、B、C.
- **Place the punctuation mark by what is quoted.** Put it before the closing quotation mark
  when the whole sentence is quoted, after it when only part is.
  Example: 他站起来问：“老师‘比较’的‘较’怎么写？” inside; “沙呦娜啦”改天见！ outside.
- **Parenthesise forced narratives, one set per event.** Use Chinese parentheses and repeat
  them at the start and end of each event when a caption spans several; main title, episode
  title and translator credit are exempt. Example: （导演） as its own event.
- **Use the fixed pattern for on-screen person captions.** Role first in parentheses, then the
  name: （导演 约翰·史密斯）. Link an actor to the character with 饰: （费雯莉饰郝思嘉）.

## Literary and religious texts

- **Weigh a resonant Chinese coinage against transliteration.** For a new religion's name,
  choose by propagation goal and record the trade-off. Example: the Baha'i faith became
  Datongjiao, 'Religion of Great Unity', echoing the datong ideal, not the Ba-ha
  transliteration.

## Translating out of Chinese

- **Watch what a restructured sentence puts last.** Promoting the main clause and moving
  conditions to the end can leave the news in a subordinate detail. Example (zh>en): a bank's
  six purpose clauses reordered so that the 2010 commitment precedes 'so as to improve ...
  prevent/control case risks'.
- **Romanise Chinese and Korean names surname-first,** unless the person uses an established
  Western order. 宁世征 becomes Ning Shizheng; 김희선
  becomes Kim Hee-sun with the second element lowercase; North Korean names take no hyphen
  (Kim Jong Un); use established equivalents for historical and mythical figures.
- **Do not add 'As for X' to a neutral topic.** The device is contrastive and claims a contrast
  the source does not make; use ordinary clause order and accept the loss. Example: 'Animals, I
  advocate a conservation policy' turns contrastive as 'As for animals ...'.
- **Supply a subject in every English clause.** Choose by last-mentioned participant, then
  participant in focus, then logic or context; if the Chinese is indeterminate, bracket the
  minimal guess rather than naming an agent silently.
- **Expect Chinese-to-English LLM drafts to keep source structures.** Check for
  under-nominalisation (Chinese verbs left as English verbs) and other places where English
  expects a recast.
- **Where machine output between Chinese and a language other than English is weak, try
  pivoting through English** and compare the two results before polishing the direct one. In
  one study Chinese to German roughly doubled its score that way.
