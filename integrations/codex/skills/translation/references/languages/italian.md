# Italian notes

Into Italian, mostly from English, with a few points on Italian into English. The typographic
conventions come from subtitling style guides and a client's guide overrides them; the rest
come from translation textbooks and studies of machine output. Coverage is slight: this is
not a style guide for Italian.

## Punctuation and typography

- **Place the full stop by the scope of the quotation.** Inside the closing quotation mark
  when the whole sentence is quoted, outside when only part of it is:
  '"Sono felicissima!"' inside; 'Disse: "Sono molto fiero di noi".' outside.
- **Never stack punctuation.** Choose the single mark that carries the intonation or the
  meaning: 'Non ci credo…' or 'Non ci credo!', not 'Non ci credo…!'.

## Numbers, dates and units

- **Write times of day in 24-hour form.** Colon, no AM/PM, no leading zero for one to nine,
  and the minutes dropped where space requires: '23:45', '1:10', 'alle 17'.
- **Use the established abbreviations.** dott. and dott.ssa (dr. and dr.ssa are accepted
  where space is short), sig., sig.ra, sig.na, and mq and kmq, not m² and km².

## Grammar carried over from the source

- **Take a person's gender from the preceding context,** not from the profession's
  stereotype, and keep it through the passage: 'Hasanova era la brigadiera capo…', not 'il
  brigadiere capo'.
- **Do not make a mixed group feminine.** The masculine plural covers a mixed or unknown
  group; a feminine plural asserts that every member is female. A neutral English 'they' is
  masculine unless the source establishes otherwise.
- **Keep action and agent; do not nominalise them away.** A clause of action or obligation is
  a commitment; a nominalised topic labels a state of affairs. 'Ensuring that all girls are
  educated' turned into 'il problema dell'istruzione femminile' has lost the commitment.

## Technical and academic texts

- **Make engagement impersonal.** English 'we' and 'you' in exposition become impersonal
  constructions: 'Now we start at point P and go through A to Q to find…' becomes
  'Percorrendo il circuito … si trova…'.
- **Turn a question that only introduces a topic into a statement.** 'But this raises the
  question: What are the appropriate boundaries for a particular firm?' becomes 'si pone il
  problema di stabilire quali siano…'.

## Legal texts, Italian into English

- **Render Latin tags; do not copy them.** Latin that is current in Italian legal writing is
  mostly not current in English. 'Legale rappresentante pro tempore' is 'acting legal
  representative'; 'salvis juribus' is 'without prejudice to any further right'.

## Subtitles and captions

- **In captions for deaf and hard-of-hearing viewers, write the action, not the noise.** The
  brackets already mark a sound: '[passi sulle foglie]', not '[rumore di passi sulle foglie]'.

## Machine output, Italian into English

- **Check canonical texts for seams.** Machine translation of a much-translated work can
  splice lines from different published translations into one passage, and it does not
  attempt metre or rhyme. The opening of Dante's Commedia came out with its first line from
  one published version and its second from another.
