# Japanese notes

Into Japanese from English and out of Japanese into English (bullets marked 'Into English'). The points on reading Japanese come from studies of machine translation of Japanese public-information texts and from translation textbooks; the typographic ones from subtitling style guides, which a client's guide overrides. This is not a complete style guide for Japanese.

## Punctuation and typography

- **Into English: treat comma-separated Japanese nouns as ambiguous.** Settle it from the
  predicate: the comma may enumerate or mark an apposition. Example: 建物の設計、施工に問題がある
  is an enumeration, "a problem with the design and the construction of the building".
- **Into English: complete every item in a coordinated list.** Where items share a head noun
  written once, 新宿・渋谷・豊島区にお住まいの方 is "residents of Shinjuku City, Shibuya City
  and Toshima City", not a list with the head noun on the last item only.

## Numbers, dates and units

- **Choose the calendar system by topic proximity.** Japanese history takes the Japanese era
  (Meiji 2, not 1869); a text about a foreign culture may keep the Western date, as a leaflet
  on a British car museum keeps 1986.
- **Into English: make the "per" relation explicit in rates.** Japanese sets quantity and price
  side by side and a bare rendering reads as a total. Example: 子ども1人1000円 is "1000 yen
  per child".

## Grammar carried over from the source

- **Do not translate passive to passive.** Identify its function (suppressing the agent,
  distancing the writer, marking adversity) and use the target's own device: active with a
  generic subject, reflexive or impersonal. Japanese passive mainly marks adversity.
- **Into English: check the named agent is still the subject.** Smooth output built on an
  invented speaker or an unstated subject is plausible, wrong and hard to notice. Example:
  地域住民が分かりやすく議題を説明しました rendered as "I explained the agenda in an
  easy-to-understand local residents."
- **Into English: give a copula nominalisation a real verb.** Do not render it as an equation
  between nouns. Example: 入場券は4月22日（金）午前10時販売開始です becomes "Tickets go on sale
  at 10 a.m. on 22 April".
- **Into English: verify polarity in bound negative constructions.** Where the negation comes
  from しか…ない, ほかならない or なければならない, copying the surface negation inverts the
  meaning. Example: 指定ごみ袋しか使えません means "you can use only the specified garbage bags".
- **Into English: restate a double-negative rule positively.** Say what must be done or what is
  allowed (ないと…できない, ないわけではない, せざるを得ない). Example: 登録しないと使用できません
  becomes "To use it, you need to register".
- **Into English: resolve れる/られる before translating.** Decide whether it is passive,
  potential or honorific and mark that reading overtly. Never carry an honorific form into
  English.
- **Into English: treat a continuing clause-final が as "and".** Use an adversative only where
  the clauses genuinely contrast. Example: 市役所は平日に開いていますが、第二土曜日も
  開いています。 becomes "The city hall is open on weekdays and on the second Saturday".
- **Into English: decide one reading when attachment is ambiguous.** Where a relative clause or
  の-linked expression can attach to more than one head, or が, の, で, と, に can carry more than
  one relation, make it explicit.

## Words and phrases

- **Into English: do not translate a fixed category label compositionally.** Where the parts
  are not separately meaningful, look up the published equivalent. Example: 燃やすごみ
  becomes "Combustibles", not "Burned trash".
- **Into English: give a light verb one concrete meaning.** Where かかる, ある, する with a noun
  or 〜てくる/〜ていく carries the main content, do not reproduce the vagueness. Example:
  リサイクルのための費用がかかってきます becomes "You will have to pay the cost for recycling".

## Address, politeness and tone

- **Do not default to women's-language markers in female dialogue.** Calibrate politeness,
  assertiveness and final particles to the character: drop polite verb forms and strongly
  feminine particles, omit watashi unless needed, prefer plain forms. De-feminisation, not
  emphasised femininity, is the corrective move in Japanese.
- **Into English: make the reader the actor in a procedure.** Example: 外国人登録証明書を
  紛失したとき becomes "When you lose a Certificate of Alien Registration", not the passive
  "When a Certificate of Alien Registration is lost".
- **Into English: render third-person self-reference as second or first person.** Where a
  speaker names the addressee or themselves by name or title, use the person the exchange makes
  clear. Example: 「古倉さんは毎日勤務なのに手を抜かないからねー！」 becomes "You work every day,
  but you never slack off!"
- **Into English: read a notice's volitional form as a directive.** 〜しよう (ごみは持ち帰ろう)
  addresses the reader: use an imperative or "Please …", not a first-person-plural suggestion.
  Example: ごみは持ち帰ろう becomes "Please take your garbage home".

## Sentences and text organisation

- **Into English: close the sentence before an inserted enumeration.** Where a case particle
  after a mid-sentence list governs a final predicate, state the predicate first and list
  afterwards.

## Software and technical texts

- **Into English: expand three or more stacked Japanese nouns.** Leave two-noun compounds
  alone; three or more are a compression whose relations (subject, object, purpose, origin)
  must be explicit. Example: 記念品代相当分 is "the amount equivalent to the cost of the
  commemorative gift".

## Legal and official texts

- **Into English: form each procedural slot by its function.** Titles as noun phrases,
  conditions as conditional clauses with an explicit "you", steps as bare imperatives. Example:
  以下の書類を持参します becomes "Bring the following documents".
- **Into English: give ものとする and こととする no lexical content.** Render the obligation or
  decision directly; the phrases are common in municipal documents and add length and
  vagueness.

## Subtitles and dubbing

- **Put speaker IDs and sound effects in full-width parentheses.** For a voice arriving through
  a device, give the medium and speaker separated by a half-width colon （電話:大介）, and keep
  source and sound in one label （マイクの泣き声）, not two.
- **Count subtitle length in full-width units.** The limit is 13 per line horizontally and 11
  vertically, each half-width character, space or punctuation mark counting as half. Example:
  eight full-width characters plus four half-width ones make ten units.
- **Replace 、 and 。 with spaces in subtitles.** A half-width space replaces the comma and a
  full-width space the stop, keeping them only inside the translation of an official title. Set
  ？ and ！ full-width, followed by a full-width space before the next sentence on the same line.
  Example: 「まさか？　そんな！」
- **Use the two-em dash for a continuing sentence.** The dash shows a sentence continuing into
  the next subtitle, at most twice per sentence, so that no sentence runs past three events.
- **Format numerals by digit count and layout.** Single digits full-width, two digits
  half-width and horizontal as tate-chū-yoko, three or more full-width in vertical layout,
  large numbers grouped by 万. Example: 12,345 becomes １万 2345 horizontally.
- **Limit kanji to what the target age can read.** First-grade kanji for ages 0-6, sixth-grade
  for ages 7-12, nothing outside the official school list; replace a rare kanji with its kana
  spelling or a simpler synonym.

## Literary and religious texts

- **Do not copy every "he" or "she" as kare/kanojo.** Omit them or substitute a name or status
  noun such as seinen, reserving kare/kanojo for specific reference, and apply one strategy
  throughout. Keeping nearly all of them reads as foreign.
- **Check that pronoun reduction has not made narration a monologue.** Omission plus inserted
  first-person forms such as jibun can turn third-person narration into a first-person voice.

## Translating out of Japanese

- **Treat long Japanese sentences as the place where drafts go wrong.** Beyond about 50
  characters, with several verbs or with a list in the middle, split at clause boundaries
  before translating and check each part against the source.
- **Supply a subject in every English clause.** Choose by last-mentioned participant, then
  participant in focus, then logic or context; where the source is indeterminate, mark the
  guess in brackets, as with "[Your Majesty]", "he" and "the government".
- **Do not report an omitted subject for a missing particle.** A noun phrase can be the subject
  with no が or は. Example: 実行委員会一同努力しています means "The executive committee is working
  hard as one".
