# Korean notes

A handful of points on the English-Korean pair from translation textbooks and a subtitling
style guide. The sources behind this skill say little about Korean, so this is not a style
guide. Korean drops subjects and marks topics much as Japanese does: the points on supplying
subjects and on topic marking in `japanese.md` and `chinese.md` apply here too.

## Into Korean

- **Move the attribution in news.** English names the source of a report in the first
  sentence; Korean news holds it back, typically to the start of the second paragraph. '…, a
  research group said on Friday' moves there as 'According to the … Institute'.
- **Delete the identifying gloss English news attaches to names.** English appends phrases
  such as 'the world's second-largest chipmaker' to companies and places; Korean leaves
  well-known names unglossed.

## Into English

- **Add the defining phrase an English reader needs** to a company, place or institution the
  Korean source leaves bare.
- **Romanise names surname-first.** South Korean given names are hyphenated with the second
  element in lower case (김희선: Kim Hee-sun); North Korean given names take no hyphen (Kim
  Jong Un). Use the established form for historical and mythical figures, and a person's own
  preferred romanisation where one is known.
- **Keep a neutral topic non-contrastive.** Where the topic particle marks a topic without
  contrast, use ordinary English clause order. 'As for animals, I advocate a conservation
  policy' claims a contrast the source need not make.
