# Russian notes

Into Russian from English, and out of Russian into English (bullets marked 'Into English'). The conventions for typography, software and tone come from localisation and subtitling style guides, which a client's own guide overrides; the points on the pair come from Russian translation textbooks.

## Punctuation and typography

- **Match the dash to its function.** The sentence dash is spaced, a range takes an unspaced
  en dash, a compound a hyphen: «См. стр. 10–20.», «интернет-магазин».
- **Use chevrons for quotations,** in print, software and web text alike. A quotation inside
  one takes straight double quotes; single quotes are never used: «кнопка "Готово"».
- **Replace the colon after attention words.** Write «Внимание!» for 'Caution:' and a full stop
  after Примечание; capitalise after a colon only before a list or direct speech.
- **Do not comma both sides of например.** An unattached оборот takes no comma after it, and по
  умолчанию takes none: «…, например дюймы или сантиметры».
- **Do not italicise titles.** Put works in chevrons and keep italics for emphasis, lyrics,
  poetry and voice-over: роман «Что делать?».

## Numbers, dates and units

- **Reverse the number separators.** Decimal comma, thousands space from five digits, no space
  before %; spell out percentages below ten: 23,99; 10 000; 25%; три процента.
- **Into English: convert Russian numerals into English form.** The decimal comma becomes a
  point, the thousands space a comma: 233 800 ДТП gives 233,800 accidents.

## Capitalisation, spelling and word formation

- **Use sentence case in headings and titles.** Capitalise the first word only, leave глава and
  раздел lowercase, and capitalise only a product name such as Календарь.

## Grammar carried over from the source

- **Invent nothing the source leaves open.** Where Russian forces number, gender or aspect,
  follow the co-text or ask: 'A friend came over' forces друг or подруга.
- **Do not mirror a passive with a passive.** Use the Russian device for the same function,
  usually active or impersonal: 'Papers are invited' gives 'We invite you to take part'.
- **Keep the user gender-neutral.** Use plural nouns and verbs, name roles, and put a role noun
  before a placeholder: «Пользователь {user} ответил на ваше сообщение».
- **Use a деепричастие only with a same-subject main clause.** Rewrite a dangling English
  participle as a когда or пока clause: Разговаривая по телефону, Ваня ужинал.
- **Treat the perfective past as marked.** Habitual and process actions take the imperfective:
  'He used to read in the evening' gives читал, not прочитал.
- **Do not give a continuing action a past verb.** Russian uses the present with a duration:
  Олег живет в Москве три года is 'has been living'.

## Words and phrases

- **Do not render 'friend' as друг by default.** Друг is for very close friends; use приятель,
  приятельница, знакомый or знакомая for casual ties.
- **Turn 'make sure' into an action verb.** Write the action: «включите автосохранение», not
  «убедитесь, что автосохранение включено».
- **Never render 'excited' with возбуждать.** The family means arousal or loss of control;
  reformulate: «Здорово, что тебя взяли на работу».
- **Prefer the everyday word.** Write сейчас, потом, распечатать, настроить, люди, указанный;
  avoid иметь место, присутствовать and вывести на печать.
- **Keep the verb, not a verb plus verbal noun.** удалить, подключиться, настроить, not
  выполнить удаление; «Вы хотите отменить удаление?».
- **Into English: split рука and нога by the anatomical detail.** рука is hand or arm and нога
  foot or leg: «Потеря руки выше локтевого сустава» is loss of the arm above the elbow.

## Address, politeness and tone

- **Do not turn 'you can' into an imperative.** «Теперь вы можете создать таблицу»; avoid вы
  должны; open help titles with «Как + verb».
- **Use neutral collectives for group address.** коллеги, все, коллектив, not дамы и господа,
  ребята or парни, which mark gender, age or rank.
- **Read the reply before translating 'How are you?'.** Answered with 'Fine, thanks' it was a
  greeting: Здравствуйте! or Как дела? Answered with real news or a complaint it was a
  question: Как у вас дела?
- **Do not copy English frequency of thanks and endearment.** Use спасибо less often, drop
  очень from Я рад(а) вас видеть, and reserve гордиться for real achievement.
- **Match 'sorry' to the speech act.** Apology, condolence and sympathy take unrelated forms:
  Извините, я не расслышал(а); Мне жалко тебя is 'I feel for you'.

## Sentences and text organisation

- **Choose и, а or но by the kind of contrast.** Но marks maximal contrast between statements,
  а links integratively and juxtaposes extremes, и plain addition: Это белое, а это черное.
- **Strip bookish connectives from consumer and help text.** Write так как, потому что, чтобы, но; not поскольку, в целях,
  с целью, однако. Sentence-initial И, Но and Или suit a conversational tone.

## Names, terms and abbreviations

- **Into English: pick one transliteration system and keep it.** Simplified popular for
  journalism, Library of Congress for bibliography, one symbol per letter for Slavicist work;
  keep conventional spellings.
- **Into English: restore a foreign name's own spelling.** принц Чарлз is Prince Charles,
  Ковент-Гарден Covent Garden, Лос-Анджелес Los Angeles.
- **Into English: expand the abbreviations the source leaves bare.** Gloss ДТП, УК РФ and
  Госавтоинспекция for an English reader; keep source definitions only where they help the
  target reader.
- **Into English: rebuild a Russian address.** Expand наб., ул., д., обл. and п., then reorder:
  наб. канала Грибоедова, дом 4 gives 4 Griboyedov Canal Embankment.

## Software and technical texts

- **Attach no case ending to an English product name.** Insert a category noun in the required
  case and leave the name bare; never turn a trademark into a verb.
- **Give an English acronym no case ending.** Take its gender from the head word of its Russian
  expansion: 'RAM' gives «ОЗУ», 'OS' gives «ОС».
- **Rebuild any sentence holding a placeholder.** Russian counted nouns change with the last
  digits: «Обнаружено папок: %1», «не менее %1 секунд».
- **Keep error messages impersonal.** Naming the user as the subject of a failure sounds like
  blame: «Эта копия программы еще не активирована».
- **Never render 'this' as данный.** It sounds bookish and collides with данные: «Вы хотите
  сохранить эти данные?», not «…данные сведения».
- **Do not translate every possessive.** Drop filler 'your' (подключите мышь), use свой where
  Russian prefers it, do not address the reader as пользователь, and make a system's 'we'
  impersonal.
- **Treat program components as inanimate.** Accusative like the nominative: использовать
  мастер, установить клиент, not мастера.
- **Into English: establish the technical sense of everyday words.** статья, партия, передача,
  ключ, сеть, привод, степень, ссылка, свеча and ставка carry unrelated senses.
- **Into English: restore the English formula behind calques.** Boilerplate is often itself a
  translation: 'on-site service', 'advance unit replacement'.

## Legal and official texts

- **Into English: do not borrow a common-law instrument name.** подзаконные акты is
  'non-statutory governmental regulations', not 'by-law'.
- **Into English: read общество as a legal-form element.** общество с ограниченной
  ответственностью (ООО) is limited company (OOO); ЗАО is closed joint-stock company (ZAO).
- **Into English: convert Russian units of account.** Печатный лист is about 40,000 characters
  with spaces, so give the count: 'up to 10 printer's sheets'.
- **Into English: carry the clause that pegs у.е.** The unit is fixed to a hard currency at the
  rate in the document, so translate '1 у.е. равна ___ доллару США'.
- **Into English: use the fixed formula for each legal preposition.**
  в силу 'by virtue of', на основании 'on the basis of', во исполнение 'pursuant to', при
  отсутствии 'in the absence of'.

## Subtitles and dubbing

- **Cut grammar before content.** Drop possessives where the possessor is obvious (Я поранил
  ногу) and framing such as 'I think' or 'just' before cutting nouns.
- **Write ё only where it disambiguates.** все/всё, узнаем/узнаём, сёрфинг, Киселёв, Дежнёв;
  omit it elsewhere, as most Russian print does.

## Literary and religious texts

- **Into English: give a pronoun the target noun's gender.** Russian veter is masculine but
  English needs 'it': 'No on ne ukhodil' is 'it didn't go away'.
- **Into English: keep mixed-script titles mixed.** The switch is the meaning: «The Телка»,
  «Generation П», «Облом off»; do not transliterate or translate half of it.
- **Into English: drop a Russian gloss on an English word.** The note 'Уединение, одиночество,
  уединенность (англ.)' explains the obvious to an English reader; remove it.
- **Into English: make an inverted formula visible.** «друзья народа» only means something
  against «враг народа», so gloss it or carry the echo; a plain rendering reads as praise.
- **Into English: keep Soviet realia as institutions.** Transliterate with a gloss or describe
  them: партком, профком, ректорат; 'HR department' removes the object of the satire.
- **Into English: handle discourse particles deliberately.** ну, вот, же, -то, ведь, блин:
  drop, absorb or replace each one; «Куда ехать-то?» needs the -то in the tone.
- **Into English: leave an unstated experiencer unstated.** нельзя было отвести глаз is 'it was
  impossible to take your eyes off', not 'we couldn't take our eyes off'.

## Translating out of Russian

- **Read the case after negation as definiteness.** Я автобус не видел is 'I didn't see the
  bus', Я автобуса не видела 'I didn't see a bus'.
- **Separate the bare instrumental from с + instrumental.** The bare form is the sole means,
  the с form an accompanying circumstance: Она его встретила улыбкой.
- **Carry an aspect switch in a question and answer.** An imperfective answer to a perfective
  question denies the result: 'I was reading it', not 'I have read it'.
- **Give a Russian impersonal dative an English subject.** Мне не спалось is 'I couldn't
  sleep'; Вам помочь? is 'Can I help you?'.
- **Read word order as approximation.** Нашей коллеге сорок пять лет is 45; Нашей коллеге лет
  сорок пять is about 45, and 'about' or 'some' carries it.
- **Spell out a narrower kinship term.** свекровь is only the husband's mother and невестка is
  daughter-in-law or sister-in-law.
- **Choose the English verb per collocation.** One prefixed root covers gained weight, dialled
  a number, picked up speed and gained altitude.
- **Establish the local referent of a derived noun.** восьмёрка is the No. 8 tram, the
  cafeteria or the G8, and собака in an address is '@'.
- **Treat prefix pairs and derivations as separate words.** оставить/обставить,
  передать/предать; братик, браток, братец and братан span neutral to criminal registers.
- **Match register, not force, in a culture-bound idiom.** Мы вам покажем кузькину мать is
  'We'll show you!'; 'We will bury you' reads as a threat of nuclear war.
- **Render мол, дескать and якобы.** They mark an unendorsed claim: 'he was, supposedly,
  writing his second book'.
- **Use the established press-release formulas.** разделяет озабоченность 'shares the concern',
  выражает готовность к сотрудничеству 'stands ready to cooperate'.
